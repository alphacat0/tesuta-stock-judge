## Core Philosophy & Universal Rules
- **Risk Exclusion & Management First:** For short-term, close all positions same day — eliminates overnight/gap risk. "Risk wo haijo suru" (exclude risk). Strict, immediate loss cutting. Pre-define invalidation. Treat cuts as "processing" not failure. Avoid "コツコツドカン" (accumulate small wins then suffer big loss).
- **Only High-Probability Setups:** "勝てる場所だけ参加する" — Participate only where reasons to win clearly outweigh reasons to lose. Stay out if edge unclear or conditions poor (low volume, thin board, fading momentum, exhausted materials).
- **Adaptability & Multiple Methods:** No single holy grail. Markets change; once-effective methods stop working when popularized. Maintain 100+ "drawers" of techniques. Add new ones yearly. Don't cling to past successes. Update continuously.
- **Capital Dictates Style:** Smaller capital → focus short-term scalping/day for experience & efficiency. Larger capital → shift toward medium-long, dividends, themes (small caps/scalping impractical as your size moves price or liquidity issues). Adjust fighting style with funds.
- **Liquidity & Order Flow Awareness:** Prefer stocks with sufficient board thickness/volume for desired lot size without excessive slippage or algo interference. On very thick boards/large lots: algos can block good fills; small lots easier for best price. Monitor this dynamically.
- **Psychology & Self-Mastery:** Day trading = fight against own fatigue, emotions, FOMO, revenge trading. Need trained quick reflexes, discipline to cut instantly. Trade only when mentally fresh. Concentration required; can be exhausting.
- **Learning Style:** Started with day trading books + successful scalpers' blogs. For longer-term: followed practitioners on Twitter/X, talked to friends doing it, learned gradually via real practice (not just theory/books which he found hard/sleep-inducing). "実戦を通じて少しずつ" (little by little through real battle).
- **No Economic Prediction:** "景気は読めないので予想しない" — Doesn't try to forecast macro economy. Focus on reactive reading or specific stock catalysts.

## Short-Term Trading (Scalping / Day Trading) - His Foundation
**Focus:** Current **需給 (supply/demand / order flow)** reading. React to what is happening now via board (板), tape/walk values (歩み値), volume (出来高), price reactions. Not prediction or heavy FA.
**Typical Stocks:** Theme stocks with sudden attention/volume surge, decent liquidity (thicker boards preferred for ease, but manageable lots). Examples from past: Laox, Sakusa HD, Kennedy, Aiful, Fintech, OncoTherapy; recent: Kioxia (キオクシア) concentrated day trades.
**Entry Signals (Positive Supply/Demand for Longs):**
- Volume surges, especially on up moves or post-news/decision.
- Selling boards being eaten/consumed sequentially.
- Continuous or sustained market buys (成行買い).
- Quick rebounds from dips (immediate support).
- Highs holding with light selling or thin offers.
- Materials/attention drawing sustained interest.
- Sufficient room to target before potential resistance.
**Avoid / Negative Signals:**
- Low or fading volume.
- Thin boards or inconsistent flow.
- Rises immediately sold off hard.
- Dips not supported, repeated new lows despite materials.
- Exhausted moves, profit taking dominant.
**Execution Notes:**
- High frequency: 100s trades/day possible in peak scalping.
- Lot sizing critical: Start small to learn fills; scale only with clear edge. Large lots on certain boards lead to unfavorable fills (buy high/sell low effectively due to algos).
- Use familiar platforms for intuitive "feel" (e.g. he uses SBI app on mobile, Rakuten MarketSpeed on PC — habit matters).
- Close all by market end. Can do concentrated on 1 stock sometimes.
- Even on phone (suboptimal) possible but harder.
**Risk/Position:** Very tight mental stops. Small wins accumulate; cut fast on invalidation. Position size so that bad fills don't destroy edge.

## Medium to Long-Term Investing (Added ~2016+)
**Focus:** Future **fund flow reasons** — catalysts that can drive buying over weeks/months/years. Combine with supply/demand for better entry timing.
**Key Elements:**
- Company performance/earnings growth trajectory.
- Positive materials/news, themes, sector tailwinds, "easy money" flowing sectors.
- Dividends (high yield when market weak for long holds).
- Undervaluation on dips with holding thesis.
- Decisions (M&A, new biz, management change) that can re-rate stock.
**Entry:** When catalyst clear + reasonable risk/reward + supply/demand supportive for timing. Not forced.
**Holding:** Weeks to years; permanent for some high quality or dividend. Re-evaluate if thesis breaks.
**Learning He Did:** Started with beginner FA books (found hard), then real practice + observing other medium-long traders on X and friends. Blends his supply/demand strength with FA.

## Position Sizing & Capital Management
- **Core Rule:** Change battle method with capital amount.
- Small capital: High-frequency short-term to build edge/experience quickly.
- Growing capital: Reduce reliance on pure scalping (limits hit); add longer holds, dividends, diversify approaches.
- Always size appropriately to liquidity: Oversized positions = slippage, poor exits, potential to move thin stocks adversely.
- Never risk too much on one idea; preserve capital for many opportunities ("コツコツ" steady accumulation).

## Practical Output for Judgment
When judging a stock:
- Specify primary/secondary timeframes.
- Quantify edge: Supply/demand strength or catalyst quality + conviction level.
- Explicit entry trigger, stop/invalidation, target logic (e.g. scale out on strength).
- Position size recommendation (conservative if any doubt on liquidity/edge).
- What would make you stay out or exit early.
- Note adaptability: Is this setup in a "crowded" popular style currently?
